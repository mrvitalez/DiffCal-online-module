✎Problems

  1. (One-to-one functions, §6.1) Decide whether each function is one-to-one. Justify each answer with a specific pair of inputs, the Horizontal Line Test, or the sign of the derivative.
    (a) \(f(x) = x^4 - 2\), \(x\) any real number   (b) \(g(x) = 5 - 2x^3\)   (c) \(h(x) = x + \dfrac1x\), \(x > 0\)

  2. (Finding an inverse, §6.1) Let \(f(x) = \dfrac{4x - 1}{2x + 3}\). Find a formula for \(f^{-1}(x)\), state the domain of \(f^{-1}\) and the range of \(f^{-1}\), and verify that \(f(f^{-1}(x)) = x\).

  3. (Graphs of inverses, §6.1) Let \(f(x) = \sqrt{9 - x^2}\) for \(0 \le x \le 3\). (a) Sketch the graph of \(f\) and describe it in words. (b) Show that \(f^{-1} = f\). (c) Explain, using the graph and the line \(y = x\), why this makes sense.

  4. (Derivative of an inverse, §6.1) Let \(f(x) = x^5 + x^3 + 2x\). (a) Show that \(f\) is one-to-one. (b) Find \((f^{-1})'(4)\) and \((f^{-1})'(0)\). (c) Write the equation of the tangent line to \(y = f^{-1}(x)\) at the point \((4, 1)\).

  5. (Derivatives of exponentials, §6.2) Differentiate and simplify.
    (a) \(y = e^{-x}\sin 3x\)   (b) \(y = \dfrac{e^{2x} + 1}{e^{2x} - 1}\)   (c) \(y = e^{e^x}\)

  6. (Limits, §6.2) Evaluate each limit, or say that it is \(\infty\) or \(-\infty\).
    (a) \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{e^{4x} - 3}{2e^{4x} + e^{2x}}\)   (b) \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{e^{4x} - 3}{2e^{4x} + e^{2x}}\)   (c) \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^+} e^{-1/x}\) and \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^-} e^{-1/x}\)

  7. (Tangent lines and extrema, §6.2) Let \(f(x) = x e^{2x - 2}\). (a) Find the equation of the tangent line at \(x = 1\). (b) Find the intervals where \(f\) is increasing or decreasing, and find the exact local minimum value.

  8. (Equations, §6.3) Solve exactly, then give a decimal approximation where it helps.
    (a) \(2^{x + 3} = 5^{x}\)   (b) \(\ln x + \ln(x - 3) = \ln 4\)   (c) \(e^{2x} - 5e^{x} + 6 = 0\)

  9. (Laws, change of base and graphs, §6.3) (a) Write \(\tfrac12\ln(x + 4) - 3\ln x + \ln 5\) as a single logarithm. (b) Use the change-of-base formula to evaluate \(\log_5 30\) to four decimal places. (c) Sketch \(y = \ln(x - 2) + 1\) by transforming the graph of \(y = \ln x\). State its domain, its vertical asymptote and its \(x\)-intercept.

  10. (Derivatives of logarithms, §6.4) Differentiate.
    (a) \(y = \ln\big(x\sqrt{x^2 + 1}\big)\)   (b) \(y = \log_2(3x^2 - x)\)   (c) \(y = 7^{\tan x}\)   (d) \(y = \ln|\cos x|\)

  11. (Logarithmic differentiation, §6.4) Use logarithmic differentiation to find \(y'\).
    (a) \(y = x^{\ln x}\), \(x > 0\)   (b) \(y = \dfrac{(x - 1)^4\sqrt{x + 2}}{(x^2 + 3)^3}\)

  12. (Applied: cooling, §6.2–6.3) A cup of hot chocolate is left on a table. Its temperature after \(t\) minutes is \(T(t) = 22 + 68e^{-0.05t}\) degrees Celsius. (a) Find \(T(0)\) and \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty} T(t)\), and explain what each means. (b) Find the rate of change of temperature at \(t = 10\) min. (c) When does the temperature reach 50 °C?

  13. (Applied: growth, §6.4) A bacteria culture starts with 500 cells and doubles every 3 hours, so the population after \(t\) hours is \(N(t) = 500\cdot 2^{t/3}\). (a) Find \(N(6)\). (b) Find \(N'(t)\) and the growth rate at \(t = 6\) hours, exactly and to the nearest whole number. (c) Show that \(N'(t)\) is a constant multiple of \(N(t)\) and find the constant.

  14. (Explain, §6.1 and §6.4) The natural logarithm is the inverse of \(f(x) = e^x\). Use the formula \((f^{-1})'(a) = \dfrac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(a))}\) to show that \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x = \dfrac1x\). Then explain carefully why \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln|x| = \dfrac1x\) also holds for \(x < 0\).

  15. (Prove, §6.4) (a) Starting from the fact that the derivative of \(\ln x\) at \(x = 1\) equals 1, prove that \(\displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}(1 + h)^{1/h} = e\). (b) Use part (a) to evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1 + \frac3n\right)^{2n}\). Explain each step.
